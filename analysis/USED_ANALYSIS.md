  1. COE-adjusted depreciation (body-only depreciation)
  The COE is a flat sunk cost — the real "car value" is price minus remaining COE.
  If we estimate the COE component from coe_left × current COE price ÷ 10, we can
  isolate how fast the car body is depreciating vs the COE. This separates the
  government tax from the actual vehicle value decay.
  
  2. Days on market
  posted_date tells us how stale a listing is. Cars sitting > 30 days likely
  overpriced — a useful inverse signal.

  3. Direct-owner discount
  Compare median prices for the same brand/model between direct-owner and dealer
  listings. Quantifies the dealer markup.

  4. Price per owner
  Price ÷ number of owners. More hands = more wear, should trade at a discount.
  Useful as a secondary quality signal.

  5. Depreciation per km driven
  Annual depreciation ÷ annual mileage. Tells you the "cost per km" of ownership — a
   real-world value metric.

  Top pick: #1 (body-only depreciation) is the most insightful — it removes the COE
  noise and reveals which cars actually hold their mechanical value. Want me to add
  any of these?
